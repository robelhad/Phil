Our Story

RoHa Philly Steak was built around one idea: a great steak needs very little. A thick cut, a cast-iron pan, high heat, and the patience to leave it alone. Everything else on 10th Avenue, the brass light, the dark wood, the quiet between courses, exists to protect one moment: the knife going through the crust.

The cut

It begins long before the pan. We choose each cut for what it will do under heat: marbling that bastes the meat from the inside, a thickness that lets the crust form while the center stays rosy, and a clean, beefy flavor that needs no disguise. The Manhattan Steak, the NY Stripe, the bone-in Ribeye, and the lamb chops each earn their place for a different reason.

The list is short on purpose. When a kitchen does a few things, it can do each of them every night, the same way, to the minute.

A short menu is a promise.

The fire

Our steaks are seared hard in cast iron, the way New York chophouses have done it for generations. The pan holds heat like nothing else, so the crust forms in seconds and the juices stay where they belong. Near the end, butter goes in with thyme and garlic, and the cut is spooned and turned until it glistens.

Then comes the part that takes the most discipline: rest. A steak left to settle for a few minutes carves cleanly and eats better, and we will not rush it, even when the table is hungry.

Leave it alone. Let the pan do the talking.

The room

Step in off 10th Avenue and the city drops away. Dark wood, deep leather, brass fixtures, and light kept low and warm, close to candlelight. We wanted the feeling of the old New York steakhouse, where the tables are generous, the booths are deep, and no one is trying to move you along.

The details are quiet: heavy linen, glassware that rings, a lamp at just the right height so everyone looks their best. You may not notice any of it, and that is the idea.

The best rooms disappear around the meal.

A Valance

Behind the main dining room is A Valance, a private room of 1,000 square feet with its own long table, candlelight, and a wall of wine. It was made for the evenings people remember: weddings, receptions, rehearsal dinners, and celebrations that deserve a door that closes.

We build the night with you. Menus are written for the occasion, wines are paired to the courses, and a dedicated team looks after the room so the host can be a guest.

A door that closes, and a table long enough for everyone.

An evening at RoHa

  1. Arrival

    You are greeted by name, coats are taken, and you are walked to a table that is already set. No one asks you to wait at the bar unless you want to.

  2. The first pour

    Wine arrives while you read. If you would like to talk it through, we are happy to. If not, we will leave you to it.

  3. The sear

    Your cut goes to the pan and the kitchen goes quiet. You may hear it from the room: the sizzle, the butter, the thyme.

  4. The carve

    Steaks to share are carved at the table, rested and sliced across the grain, with the pan juices spooned over.

  5. The last glass

    Dessert, coffee, or just one more pour. The table is yours for as long as you like.

What we believe

Few things, done well.

A focused menu, cooked the same way every night.

Heat and patience.

Sear hard, rest properly, and never hurry either one.

Hospitality without hurry.

Your table is yours for the whole evening.
